Catch hearing change early, before it reaches speech frequencies

Record a baseline before treatment and a test before each cycle. Every test is compared with the baseline using the ASHA (1994) criteria for a significant threshold shift, including the extended high frequencies where early change appears first.

Patients

How the comparison works

A significant threshold shift (ASHA, 1994) is any one of the following, compared with baseline, in either ear:

  1. A. A shift of 20 dB or more at any one test frequency.
  2. B. A shift of 10 dB or more at two adjacent test frequencies.
  3. C. Loss of response at three consecutive test frequencies where there was a response at baseline.

A shift should be confirmed by a retest, ideally within 24 hours, before it is reported as ototoxic change. Report confirmed change to the treating oncologist or physician promptly; the decision to change treatment is theirs.

Good practice: test before the first dose (or as soon as possible after it for aminoglycosides), before each chemotherapy cycle or regularly during aminoglycoside treatment, and after treatment ends. Include extended high frequencies and otoacoustic emissions where available.

Sources: American Speech-Language-Hearing Association. Audiologic management of individuals receiving cochleotoxic drug therapy [Guidelines]. ASHA 1994;36(Suppl 12):11–19. American Academy of Audiology. Position statement and clinical practice guidelines: Ototoxicity monitoring. 2009.
